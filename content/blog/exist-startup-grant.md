We were almost ready to apply for exist, one of Germany's best startup grants. The university's startup center and our professor had nearly approved our idea paper, and we had letters of intent from potential customers.

Then we made our first sale, and that disqualified us.

Here's what exist offers, the two rules that can rule you out, and what I'd do differently.

## What exist gives you

The exist Gründungsstipendium (formerly "Gründerstipendium") is a federal startup grant from the German Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs (BMWE). It runs for 12 months and pays each founder (up to three) a monthly stipend:

| Founder | Per month |
|---|---|
| PhD | €3,000 |
| University graduate (Bachelor or Master) | €2,500 |
| Vocational qualification | €2,000 |
| Student | €1,000 |

On top of that you get:

- **€30,000 for hardware and equipment** the business needs (up to €10,000 for a solo founder)
- **€5,000 for coaching**, plus €150 per child per month
- **A mentor and a place to work** at the university, including access to labs and infrastructure

The university applies on your behalf, and your mentor (usually a professor, or a research group leader) has to be employed there.

**Who can apply:** students who have finished at least half of their degree, and graduates within five years of graduating. At least one person on the team needs a university degree, and there are a few more team-mix rules on [exist.de](https://exist.de/programm/gruendungsstipendium/). The idea has to be genuinely innovative, with real R&D, not just opening a store or a barbershop.

Applying alone is allowed, but everyone we talked to suggested three founders, ideally with someone from a business background. That isn't an official rule, just the advice we got.

## The 2 things that can rule you out

### 1. You can't have registered a company or started selling

The company can't be founded, and business activity can't have started, before the funding begins. You're allowed to found the company and start selling *during* the funding year, as long as it's kept strictly separate from the funded project at the university.

So the usual online advice, "register a company as soon as you start a business", can cost you this grant.

### 2. You can't have a job

The stipend is meant to be what you live on. While you're funded, you can't be employed or receive another stipend or funding that covers your living costs. Paid side work is capped at 20 hours per month. If your employer agrees to pause your contract (a "founder sabbatical"), that's allowed.

If you have a full-time job, getting the grant means leaving it or pausing it. Plan for that before you apply.

## How the application works

1. Talk to your university's startup center (Gründungsnetzwerk). They help with the application and with finding a mentor.
2. Write an idea paper (Ideenpapier) of at most 15 pages. It isn't a full business plan: it covers the idea, the innovation, the team, the market, your work plan and some simple financial planning. Letters of intent can be attached on top.
3. The university submits it. There are no deadlines, and the review takes several weeks.

## Our story

We were applying with [NISD2](https://nisd2.eu), our NIS2 compliance project. We didn't have a third co-founder yet, but the idea paper was nearly approved.

One piece of feedback from the professor was that we didn't need to forecast revenue in the funding year, which our draft did. Maybe I misunderstood him, because the official template asks for a simple profit and loss plan up to break-even. Either way, not planning to make money in the first year felt counterintuitive to us.

So we decided to try to earn some revenue. If it worked, we wouldn't apply, since we'd no longer qualify, but the revenue itself would hopefully be a good signal.

There was a complication too. We had already founded a UG with a generic name and a generic purpose ("development and delivery of software services"), so that our other mini apps and websites could go under it as well. We told the startup center from the beginning, and they were a bit worried. The UG is in the NISD2 imprint for liability protection, so the connection is public.

We built a paid offer, got our first order and sent the invoice. Our guarantee gives customers 30 days to pay: if you don't like it, cancel the invoice and don't pay.

Then the money landed in the bank account… Juhuu!

Okay, now we really don't qualify for exist. Now we need to make more sales!

## What I'd do differently

I had heard about exist before starting NISD2, but it didn't occur to me to wait before registering a company. Most advice says to register as soon as possible so you can get paid and have liability protection, and that's what we did. (If you do decide to register, here's [every step it took us, with costs and timelines](/).)

If you're in Germany, have an innovative R&D idea, and graduated within the last five years, hold off on registering a company or making your first sale until you've decided whether to apply for exist.

exist alumni get a lot of credibility with companies and customers. It's well known, well respected, and one of the best programs out there for early startups. If you get the chance to apply and win, I wish you great success.
