What if every compulsory compliance law had an easy walkthrough, like TurboTax does for taxes?

The NIS2 directive replaced the first NIS directive and reaches far more of the economy. The first one covered operators of essential services and some digital service providers. NIS2 widened the list of sectors and pulled in medium-sized companies, from 50 employees (or €10 million in annual turnover) up. Many of them have neither the budget nor the staff to implement a security law from scratch.

That's where our idea came from: a walkthrough that takes a company through NIS2 step by step. A TurboTax for NIS2. We called it [NISD2](https://nisd2.eu).

## From "good idea" to a paying customer

The idea resonates with the community and the people we discuss it with. It took us a while to earn real money, and with it real validation, but someone has now paid for our software. So we've got that going for us, which is nice.

But there's a difference between people saying "that's a good idea" and real product-market fit.

## What is product-market fit?

As I understand it: companies in the market want the product and will pay for it. If they don't need it or won't buy it, it doesn't fit.

To be honest, I need to learn more about the topic myself. It seems like a hard problem to find and solve, and it could be a good research direction: finding product-market fit systematically, not only through surveys.

## Why NIS2 is an odd market

NIS2 is an odd market to test this in. The law creates the need, but it doesn't create the want. Many companies that are in scope don't know it yet, and many that do know would rather not think about it.

A product can fit a legal obligation perfectly and still sit on the shelf. So the question for us isn't only whether the walkthrough works, but whether a company that's avoiding NIS2 will pick it up at all.

For our product, I'm confident there is a fit. We just need to find more of the market for it to snuggle into.

## Starting at the small end, on purpose

The walkthrough's goal is to scale down to the companies at the small end of NIS2. Article 21 lets a company scale its security measures in proportion to its exposure to risk, its size, and the likelihood and severity of incidents.

It doesn't let anyone skip a requirement. All ten areas in Article 21 still apply, and some duties, like registration and incident reporting, apply in full regardless of size. What changes is the depth.

So companies of 50 to 150 employees became our ICP (ideal customer profile). The walkthrough takes them through every requirement at the depth that fits a company that size, using the German BSI's published defaults.

The TurboTax comparison works here too. In Germany there's Taxfix, which is similar to TurboTax. When I tried it recently, I couldn't use my freelance tax number. Freelance and self-employment income are more advanced tax needs, and the app wasn't built for them yet.

We'll likely need to grow the same way, once customers tell us they need more depth than the small-company track gives them.

## The mission

We don't want to limit ourselves to this version. We want to be *the* solution for NIS2.

The mission is to lower the cost of compliance for the European economy by making difficult compliance easier to do. Strict compliance is a good thing: it keeps critical and important infrastructure secure. But why not make it affordable?

Ultimately we have big goals, and we need to make money for the next step. I'm optimistic this can be that step for us. Not that we only care about money. I just mean compliance itself isn't what excites me. Creating an easy, understandable way for people to do their compliance and legal work: that I can be passionate about. That's a problem worth solving.

## Why a human stays in the loop

In this hype cycle of artificial intelligence, letting the AI do the task for you is easy. But legal compliance needs a human in the loop.

If an AI wrote your incident response plan and an incident happens, the people involved won't know how to follow it. The law also puts the responsibility on management: they have to approve the measures themselves.

We used AI to build the app, and we use it for daily operations like writing and translating emails. But the platform's structure is the NIS2 requirements themselves, broken into concrete steps and backed by the BSI's own default methods. Where we used AI to draft explanations, we check them against the primary sources: the law and the BSI.

## The version of product-market fit I trust so far

I said I'm still learning what product-market fit is. Here's the version I trust so far:

Someone who never had to care about NIS2 finishes the walkthrough, signs, and doesn't need us to explain what they signed.

One of those is a customer. One hundred of those is a market.
