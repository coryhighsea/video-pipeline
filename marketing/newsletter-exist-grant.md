**DON'T register your company or start selling early if you want to apply for the exist Startup Grant.** It's a hard requirement, and getting it wrong could leave you wishing you had decided differently.

## What exist is

The exist Gründungsstipendium (formerly "Gründerstipendium") is a great federal startup grant program from the German Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs (BMWE). It pays each founder a generous monthly stipend for 12 months. On top of that, the team gets a lump sum for hardware and other things the business needs: €30,000 for a team, or up to €10,000 for a solo founder. There's also a €5,000 coaching budget and €150 per child per month.

The university applies on your behalf, not you. Your project needs a mentor at the university who supports the idea, usually a professor, though a research group leader also works. The mentor has to be permanently employed there.

The university also has to give the team a place to work, including basic lab equipment and other infrastructure. So you can use the university's labs and get a desk or an office.

## Who gets how much

A team can have up to three founders, and each one gets a stipend based on their level of education:

| Founder | Per month |
|---|---|
| PhD | €3,000 |
| University graduate (Bachelor or Master) | €2,500 |
| Vocational qualification | €2,000 |
| Student | €1,000 |

Some rules about the team mix:
- At least one person must have a university degree.
- Students must have completed at least half of their degree, and the team shouldn't be mostly students.
- At most one person can have only a vocational qualification or a degree older than five years.

Applying alone is allowed, but everyone we talked to suggested having three founders for the best chance of acceptance. They also said it helps if someone on the team comes from a business background, not just engineering or computer science. That isn't an official criterion, just the advice we got.

## Who can apply

Students and graduates can apply. Graduates have to be within five years of graduating (or of leaving a research position). The project has to be based at a participating German university or research institution.

The project also needs to be innovative and involve real R&D, not just opening a store or a barbershop. The official wording asks for a "clearly recognizable unique selling point", and small improvements or combinations of existing products are explicitly excluded.

## The application

You write an idea paper (Ideenpapier) of at most 15 pages (Arial 12pt, 1.5 line spacing). It isn't a full business plan, just an outline of your idea, the innovation, the team, the market, your planned work and some financial planning. Letters of intent (LOIs) can be attached on top of the 15 pages.

There are no deadlines. You can apply anytime, and the review takes several weeks. Start with your university's startup center (Gründungsnetzwerk). They help with the paper and with finding a mentor, and they submit the application.

## The other big thing: you can't have a job

The stipend is meant to be what you live on, so you can't have a job while you're being funded. You also can't get another stipend or other funding that pays your living costs at the same time. Paid side work is capped at 20 hours per month. If your employer agrees to pause your contract (a "founder sabbatical"), that's allowed.

So plan for this before you apply. If you have a full-time job, receiving the grant means leaving it or pausing it.

## Our story

We started the process and were ready to apply with [NISD2](https://nisd2.eu), our NIS2 compliance project. We didn't have a third co-founder, but the University startup center and the professor had almost fully approved our idea paper. We even had a few letters of intent.

One piece of feedback from the professor after reading the draft was that we didn't need to forecast revenue in the funding year, which our draft did. Either I misunderstood him or the advice was off, because the official idea paper template asks for a simple profit and loss statement with sales planning at least until break-even. The rules actually say you can found the company and even start selling during the funding year. It just can't happen before the funding starts, and the company has to be kept strictly separate from the funded project at the university.

Either way, not planning to make money in the first year felt counterintuitive to us.

We decided to try and earn some revenue. If it worked, we wouldn't apply, since we'd no longer qualify, and the revenue itself would hopefully be a good signal.

We had already founded our UG, Kardashev Catalyst UG, under a generic name and a generic purpose ("development and delivery of software services"). It wasn't named after or tied directly to the NISD2 project. We also have other mini apps and websites, and if any of them make money they can go under this umbrella UG too.

We disclosed this to the startup center from the beginning, and they were a bit worried it would cause issues. The UG is listed on the NISD2 imprint page for liability protection, so there's a clear public connection between the two.

After that decision, we built a paid offer for the platform, got our first order and sent the invoice. Our guarantee gives customers 30 days to pay: if you don't like it, just cancel the invoice and don't pay.

Then the money landed in the bank account… Juhuu!

Okay, well, now we really don't qualify for exist, and now we need to make more sales!

## What I'd do differently

I had heard about exist before starting NISD2, but it didn't occur to me to wait before registering a company in case we wanted to apply. A lot of advice online says to register a company as soon as you decide to start a business, so you can actually get paid and have liability protection. So we did that as soon as possible, without thinking ahead about exist.

If you're in Germany, have an innovative technical R&D idea, and graduated from university within the last five years, I'd suggest holding off on registering a company or making your first sale until you've explored whether to apply for the exist grant.

exist alumni gain a lot of credibility with other companies and potential customers. It's a well-known, respected grant and one of the best out there for early startup projects.

If you get the chance to apply and win an exist grant, I wish you great success.
